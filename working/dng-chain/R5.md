**C1 (Q1a).** The only quantified clean cinemate record of 4K Clear HDR 16-bit at 25 fps is O1: eight ~10 s takes **with Log 12 on** (12.7 MB/frame), medium unstated, counters not quoted. The kb's own arithmetic says a 10 s take cannot show a steady deficit below ~1.7 % (`working/research-30fps-answer.md:19-20`). There is **no** cinemate record of a 16-bit linear (16.9 MB/frame) 4K 25 fps take of minutes that was clean; the one multi-minute attempt (O3) stalled, on NTFS/FUSE. The kb's comparison baseline for Apertar is therefore weaker than the operator's belief: the operator's memory plus O1 (short, Log on) and O2 (uncounted). The kb says so itself (`working/research-30fps/q7-cinemate.md:199-202, 324`: "Patrik's observation is not written down anywhere").

- O18. The gaps are single frames at exactly two periods, steady through the take. - O19. The back end's idle start→done was 13.4 ms at 16-bit 4K (hl:190), already 67 % of a 20 ms period; its time while recording at 12-bit 50 fps is unmeasured.
- O20. Core's copy worker reads SDR raw from **uncached** CFE buffers: `cachedRaw` is granted only for COMP1 or Clear-HDR-unpacked (CC:8196-8200). The 10-25 measurement showed CPU passes over uncached raw at ~225 MB/s (clearhdr-design.md:100). The 355 counted losses are capture gaps (sensor-timestamp holes), so they are frames the CFE never delivered, not copy-slot refusals.
- O21. Which raw transport the take used is not stated: Core's SDR default is `SRGGB12_CSI2P` (CC:2206, 12 MB/frame on the link and in the CFE write) with COMP1 (8 MB) chosen under conditions not re-read here; the kb's 11-01 entry says COMP1 for SDR lossless (hl:861). **C5 (what T34 M3 showed).** Storage was not the limiting stage in those two takes (queue 2-3/12, 0 write failures, 0 queue-full), and raw-buffer count was not either (8 vs 10 identical). The frames were lost before Core's recorder, in a steady one-per-~8-frames pattern. The kb's own wording ("probably the sensor/capture side at 3840x2076 (frame timing or CSI rate) rather than Core; unmeasured", hl:594-595) names the sensor; the better-supported reading by analogy with the measured 30 fps case is libcamera's serial per-frame cycle (back end + completion) exceeding 20 ms while recording. Both are **probable, unmeasured**: the discriminating measurement is preview-only delivery at 50 fps (`working/tools/ipa_rate.py`): 50.00 fps idle and ~44 recording means recording-added load (cycle); ~44 idle means a sensor/link/timing limit.

**C6 (can a writer-side change affect this case at all?).** Per the evidence, the four writer-side levers of the drop-frames build do not touch the loss mechanism measured in T34 M3:
- **Log**: does not apply to SDR (COMP1/packed streams record linear; hl:857-869; `planCdngLog` maps unpacked 2-byte streams only). Nothing changes.
- **ram buffer**: changes what happens when the 12-frame queue fills; it never passed 3/12. Nothing changes.
- **trim**: removes the drive stalls that show as queue growth; none was seen. It may raise the sustained ceiling toward the 570 MB/s a clean take needs (unmeasured), but it cannot create frames the CFE skipped.
- **sync policy**: the per-file fsync thread and the per-frame writeback initiation affect writeback timing; their only route to the capture side is DRAM/CPU contention (see §7), and the per-frame DONTNEED initiation is present in every configuration anyway.
The only writer-side items with a plausible route to the capture side are (i) the bytes the writer moves through DRAM (the page-cache `write()` copy, ~24 MB read+write per 12-bit frame = ~1.2 GB/s at 50 fps; O_DIRECT (T24) would remove it and was skipped, README:261-262; `working/ramp-mode-ladder.md:47-50` already predicted "4K at 50 fps needs T24"), (ii) a cached-heap copy source for SDR raw (plan row 4, shelved, README:219), and (iii) the WebUI MJPEG downsample that runs on libcamera's completion thread when a browser is attached (App:9355, :9420-9424). None of these is in the drop-frames build. **Probable** (from the counters and the code; the 50 fps back-end cycle itself is unmeasured).

The 10 % capture loss in A2 is one run and is **unexplained**.

## 9. Q9 — Does the 2026-10-26 "30 fps limit is libcamera's single CameraManager thread" finding bear on 4K 50 fps SDR?

What the finding says (hl:177-202, refined by hl:219-232 and `research-30fps-answer.md:16-36, 118-131`):
- The RPi PiSP pipeline handler runs one frame at a time through IPA + back end (`tryRunPipeline` only when `state_ == Idle`), so the per-frame serial cycle must fit the frame period. While recording 16-bit 4K at 30 fps the cycle was 36.2 ms (BE start→done 34.6 ms as a log interval), frames queued 217 ms deep, and the CFE skipped one frame per cycle overrun (steady single-frame gaps). Preview-only at 30 fps: cycle 33.3 ms, BE 13.4 ms.
- Later refinement: freeing the CameraManager thread (L2, 34 % busy) did not restore 30 fps, so the thread's own CPU is not the limit; the best-supported reading is the back end's hardware time stretched by DRAM contention that only recording adds (TDN on a 16-bit reference is 65 % of the back end's bytes). PROBABLE, not measured (`research-30fps-answer.md:21-25`, critic H1).

How it maps onto 50 fps SDR 12-bit (period 20 ms):

| Term of the serial cycle | 16-bit 4K 30 fps (measured) | 12-bit SDR 4K-crop 50 fps (inferred) | Label |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| rev16 swap on the thread | 6.1 ms idle / 9.9 ms recording (hl:188) | **absent** (16-bit only) | confirmed (code via kb reports) |
| software raw stats | 5.8 / 7.1 ms (hl:187) | **absent** (hardware stats valid at 12-bit) | confirmed (code via kb reports) |
| back end incl. TDN | 13.4 ms idle (upper bound), ~34.6 ms recording interval (hl:190) | bytes per frame ~2/3 of the 16-bit case if the CFE format is packed 12-bit (TDN reference follows the CFE format); time unmeasured; must fit **20 ms** with IPA and completion | probable (arithmetic), unmeasured |
| Core on the completion thread | metadata, preview descriptor, (MJPEG downsample if a client) | same | confirmed (code) |
| Observed delivery | 26.45 fps = 37.8 ms cycle at 30 fps requested | ~44 fps = ~22.7 ms cycle at 50 fps requested (T34 M3) | confirmed (counts), the cycle is arithmetic |

**C11.** The question's premise ("two stats passes, swap and stats per frame at 20 ms") does not apply to 12-bit SDR: the two software passes exist only for 16-bit (and 14-bit) streams. What does carry over is the structural point: one serial IPA→BE→completion cycle per frame, and a back end whose idle time (13.4 ms at 16-bit 4K) is already two thirds of a 20 ms period before recording adds DRAM traffic. The T34 M3 signature (steady single-frame skips, ~44 of 50 fps, unchanged with more buffers, queue empty) is the same signature the 30 fps analysis produced, so the finding is the most plausible explanation of T34 M3 — **probable by analogy, measured in neither the 50 fps preview nor the 50 fps take**. The two cheap measurements that would make it confirmed or refuted: (1) preview-only delivery at 50 fps 4K SDR 12-bit (`ipa_rate.py`), (2) one `RPI:0` window with `be_cycle.py` while recording at 50 fps. `working/ramp-mode-ladder.md:48-50, 70-73` already asked for exactly these and they were not run. Core cannot fix a libcamera cycle overrun by writer changes; the levers the kb lists are TDN off (tuning copy), fewer DRAM bytes (COMP1 transport, O_DIRECT, no page-cache copy), and the completion-thread work.

## 11. Assessments

### 11.1 "Did recording get better with the drop-frames changes?" — strictly as the evidence says

Measured:
- M-a. The drive-state stall (queue-full bursts after ~0.9 TB written without a trim) is real and a trim cures it: robust synthetic evidence (hl:701-734), plus one Core take each on an untrimmed (31 % lost) and a freshly trimmed (0 lost) drive (hl:736-755). Core's automatic trim at mount ran once and took 0.47 s (hl:817-818).
- M-b. Log reduces a 16-bit frame to 12,451,344 B (−25 %) (hl:820). One frame.
- M-c. The ram buffer and safety stop engage as designed (one take, hl:871-877).
- M-d. The pre-roll runs at the sensor maximum (one run, hl:842-855).

Not measured:
- N-a. No 10-minute (or 3-minute) take of either operator case on the drop-frames build (d44b177 or later). The build's only takes are four short lossy ones with the WebUI open, one 3-minute lossless take with no drop line, one Log frame, the pre-roll and the safety-stop take.
- N-b. The auto-trim acceptance test (several 10-min takes with deletes past one full pass of the drive, against `APERTAR_MEDIA_TRIM=0`) — README:128-129, open.
- N-c. Log's effect on drops over a long take; Log with the ram buffer; Log at 25 fps on a trimmed drive (hl:752).
- N-d. The 50 fps SDR case on a trimmed drive, at 3840x2160, on the drop-frames build (hl:752-753; README:133-134 asks for 60 fps).
- N-e. The capture-gap class (A2, short takes, T34 M3, 30 fps) is untouched by any drop-frames change and its cause is not established; the kb's own open item 1 (README:125) is the WebUI two-take test.

**Assessment.** For the case the operator names first (4K Clear HDR 16-bit 25 fps, even with Log), the evidence supports "the queue-full losses from a worn-in untrimmed drive are gone when the drive is trimmed" (A0, one take) and nothing more: the drop-frames build itself has not been shown clean on a long take, and its short takes lost frames at capture. For the second case (4K 50 fps SDR 12-bit) nothing in the build addresses the measured loss mechanism (§5 C6) and no new take exists. The README's one-sentence outcome ("the dropped frames were the recording drive's state, not Apertar's buffer", README:15-16) is supported for the queue-full class and not for the capture-gap class, which the same README lists as open (finding 6).

### 11.2 "Does the pre-roll have an effect?" — per the evidence

No measurement in either stack (§6). The planned A/B (README:240-257) was never run. The only stated mechanisms are the kb's guesses; one of them (APST wake) is moot on this rig (APST already disabled by boot argument). A small untested downside exists (0.7 GB of untrimmed deleted space per pre-roll, below the 1 GiB delete-trim threshold). **Unconfirmed either way; the honest default would be off until step 0b is run.**

### 11.3 Biggest evidence gaps, in order of what they would decide

1. One repeat of A2 and A1 interleaved on a trimmed drive with the WebUI closed and the Pi idle, with `ss`, `/proc/vmstat`, `top -H` and `dropped_copy_slot=` captured (§7). Decides whether plan row 2 (no in-take sync) was wrongly dropped and whether the capture-gap class is environmental.
2. Three 10-minute takes of 4K Clear HDR 16-bit 25 fps uncompressed and with Log, on the drop-frames build, trimmed drive, WebUI closed. Decides "did it get better" for the first case.
3. 4K SDR 12-bit at 50 fps: preview-only delivery (`ipa_rate.py`), then a 60 s take on a trimmed drive at 3840x2160 and at the 2076 crop, with the raw transport format and `dropped_copy_slot=` recorded, and one `RPI:0` window for `be_cycle.py`. Decides whether the loss is the libcamera cycle (not a writer problem) or something else.
4. The WebUI two-take test (README:125) and one `top -H`/`usample.py` profile during a take on the drop-frames build.
5. A counted cinemate take of each case on the same rig (q7 X1). Decides whether the comparison the operator makes has a measured baseline at all.
6. The auto-trim acceptance series (README:128-129), and a reading of whether a format without trim keeps the drive clean (the C0001 10-26 post-format take was clean on an untrimmed drive, which the trim theory alone does not predict).
7. The pre-roll A/B (README:240-257).
8. `MemTotal`/`MemAvailable` and the HDMI mode of the rig, recorded once in the kb.
