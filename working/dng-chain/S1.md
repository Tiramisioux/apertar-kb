### 3.3 Reading the 2026-10-30 stall against this (observations, then inference)

Observations (`HL:701-735`, confirmed): Core stopped; a synthetic 4-writer program at 25 files/s, 16.9 MB per file in 491,520-byte writes; after `fstrim` 9 (then 9, then 12 of 12) runs clean, worst file 26-282 ms; past ≈ 0.9 TB without a trim every layout stalled (29-77 % late, worst 5.5-10.2 s); heat not the cause; folder layout, files-created-ahead and `fallocate` preallocation made no difference (preallocation worse); 23 stack dumps of Core's writers during a bad take sat in `exfat_alloc_cluster > exfat_ent_set > __bread_gfp`, `exfat_write_inode > __wait_on_buffer`, `exfat_lookup / path_openat` "with nothing in flight on the device"; **"the same bytes written into one large file were clean"** (`HL:722-723`).

Inference (probable):
1. `exfat_ent_set` is reached only on the `ALLOC_FAT_CHAIN` path (R6 a10, a13): at the time of the dumps the frame files were **not contiguous**, i.e. after one pass of the volume (≈ 917 GiB ≈ the 909-981 GB at which the first bad run came) `clu_srch_ptr` had wrapped and allocations landed in deleted holes.
2. Each chained frame then needs up to 127 FAT-entry updates, each preceded by a **synchronous 4 KiB read** of a cold FAT sector (`__bread_slow`). A drive that is slow to answer small reads (erase-before-write backlog on never-trimmed LBAs; DRAM-less, no HMB → two NAND accesses per random read) turns 127 small reads into seconds. "One large file was clean" fits: a single large file stays `NoFatChain` (one run) and never reads the FAT; its big sequential writes just queue. So the stall was **the drive's state × exFAT's synchronous metadata reads on fragmented free space**. Trim fixed the drive side (12 of 12). Fragmentation itself persists after a trim (a trim does not defragment) and was **not** harmful in A0 at 25 fps on a trimmed drive — either the A0 clip landed in a contiguous hole (whole 10-min clips are deleted as a block) or chained files on a fast drive cost little. Unconfirmed: `sudo filefrag -v` on DNGs of a late clip (extents > 1 = chained) and `iostat -x 1 nvme0n1` during a take (`r/s > 0` on a pure-write workload = metadata misses).
3. R2's and R4's "FAT-chain after wrap" lead and the kb's "erase before write" mechanism are the same event seen from two sides; the kb's cure (trim) is right and sufficient at 25 fps; the metadata side is what 1 MiB clusters, contiguity, and an HMB would address (§11 S1-10, S1-11, S1-14).

## 4. Kernel writeback, fadvise, fsync: what each stack does and what was measured

### 4.1 Per-DNG sequence on an Apertar writer thread (confirmed, `CE:1749-1783`, `CC:4495-4510, 4976-4979`)

1. `open(path, O_WRONLY|O_CREAT|O_TRUNC|O_CLOEXEC, 0644)` — the only name lookup (kernel-side, in `open`); no `stat`/`access`.
2. `posix_fadvise(fd,0,0,POSIX_FADV_SEQUENTIAL)` — read-ahead only (`f_ra.ra_pages ×2`); no effect on writes (R6 e2).
3. `write` header (8 B); **34 × `write` of 64 rows** (491,520 B at 16-bit, 368,640 B packed 12); 0-3 pad bytes; `write` IFD (1,532 B linear; 9,736 B with Log's 8 KiB LinearizationTable). Each `write()` → `copy_from_user` per 4 KiB page (kernel copy) + `exfat_get_block` per block under `s_lock` + `balance_dirty_pages_ratelimited`.
4. **`posix_fadvise(fd,0,0,POSIX_FADV_DONTNEED)`** → `__filemap_fdatawrite_range(WB_SYNC_NONE)` over the whole file (all ≈ 4,050 dirty pages submitted as bios from this thread) → `lru_add_drain()` (local) → `mapping_try_invalidate`: **fails for every page that is dirty or under writeback** (`mapping_evict_folio` returns 0) → `nr_failed > 0` → **`lru_add_drain_all()`** (mutex; queues a work item on every CPU with pending LRU batches or buffer heads in its bh-LRU — on this workload practically all 4; the caller **sleeps until each CPU's kworker has run**) → second `invalidate_mapping_pages`, fails the same way. Net: writeback of the file is initiated; **nothing is freed** (pages stay as clean pages after I/O and are reclaimed later by the LRU); one global drain per frame. (R6 e2-e4, `mm/fadvise.c@v6.12:generic_fadvise`, `mm/truncate.c@v6.12:mapping_evict_folio`, `mm/swap.c@v6.12:__lru_add_drain_all`; probable: kernel reading, not measured on the Pi.)
5. `close` (exFAT has no flush on release).
6. `startWriteback`: `open(O_RDONLY)` + `sync_file_range(fd,0,0,SYNC_FILE_RANGE_WRITE)` + `close` — the same `__filemap_fdatawrite_range(WB_SYNC_NONE)`; with no dirty-tagged pages left it is a tag-scan no-op (probable). `APERTAR_DNG_WRITEBACK=0` turns only this off.
7. `noteWrittenFile(path)` → list for the sync thread.
8. Sync thread (`syncLoop`, `CC:4525-4549`, `APERTAR_MEDIA_SYNC_MS` default 1000): every second, for every file written in that second `open`+`fsync`+`close`, then `fsync(dirFd)`. On exFAT each `fsync` = data wait + dir-entry write + `sync_blockdev` + **device FLUSH** → **26 FLUSHes/s at 25 fps, 51/s at 50**; the metadata buffers it writes (bitmap, FAT, directory) are the ones the writers are dirtying → writers wait on buffer locks while they are under I/O (probable; this is `__wait_on_buffer` in the stall stacks).
9. At stop: writers drain the queue (every queued frame written), then `syncfs(clip folder fd)` (`CC:4554-4568`), measured 62-236 ms (`HL:412, 745, 763`).

Syscalls per frame: 40 on the writer (open, fadvise, 36 writes, fadvise, close) + 3 (`startWriteback`) + ≈ 3 (sync thread) ≈ **46**; cinepi-raw: **4** (`open`, `fadvise(SEQUENTIAL)`, one `write` of the whole DNG, `close`, `DE:2351-2379`) + one `console->info("DNG written")` stdout line that Python regex-scans.

## 7. Pre-roll from the storage side (confirmed from source; effects unmeasured)

### S1-2 — per-frame `POSIX_FADV_DONTNEED` on a file whose pages are dirty/under writeback (cause of first-seconds gaps: candidate; improvement: likely)
- Claim: every DNG costs one global `lru_add_drain_all` (a mutex plus a work item on every CPU with pending LRU batches or buffer heads, the writer sleeping until each CPU's kworker ran) and frees nothing; 25/s at 25 fps, 50/s at 50 fps, from 4 writer threads concurrently.
- Mechanism: `CE:1780` issues DONTNEED right after the last `write()`; `generic_fadvise` writes the file back (WB_SYNC_NONE) and then `mapping_try_invalidate` fails on every page under writeback → `lru_add_drain_all()` (R6 e2-e4, `mm/fadvise.c@v6.12`, `mm/truncate.c@v6.12:mapping_evict_folio`, `mm/swap.c@v6.12:__lru_add_drain_all`). The wait is bounded by the worst SCHED_OTHER kworker scheduling latency across the 4 CPUs: small on an idle box, tens of ms when a CPU is busy (WebUI HTTP threads, a browser MJPEG encoder, a boot-time fsck or trim, VS Code server). Upstream history: Yafang Shao's v5.10 fix was motivated by "random latency spikes when their RT process is running ... tens of milliseconds" from exactly this call (R6 e5).
- Evidence for: code order confirmed; kernel mechanism confirmed at function level by R6; the first-seconds gaps correlate with the WebUI open and a recent reboot (`HL:779-795`), i.e. busy CPUs. Against: A0/A1 ran 10 min clean with the same 25 drains/s (so the drain alone is not sufficient); the drain blocks the *writer*, and reaches the capture thread only through kworker wakeups and the mutex serialising 4 writers.
- Test: `sudo bpftrace -e 'kprobe:lru_add_drain_all { @c[comm] = count(); } kretprobe:lru_add_drain_all /@s[tid]/ { @lat = hist(nsecs - @s[tid]); delete(@s[tid]); } kprobe:lru_add_drain_all { @s[tid] = nsecs; }'` during a 60 s take with the WebUI open (expect ≈ 25/s from the writers and a latency histogram); then an env knob `APERTAR_DNG_DONTNEED=0` (one line at `CE:1780`) and the same take: drains → 0; count `drop[capture-gap]`.
- Expected effect: removes 25-50 cross-CPU drains/s; page cache then holds clean pages up to the LRU (harmless; `sync_file_range(WRITE)` still bounds the dirty set at ≈ one frame per writer). If the first-seconds gaps fall, this was a coupling; if not, it is still a free cost cut. Better form: DONTNEED on file N−2 after `sync_file_range(WAIT_BEFORE|WRITE|WAIT_AFTER)` on it (R6's lead), which frees the pages and never drains. Risk: low. Interaction: if Apertar ever adopts `chrt -f 70` (kb plan row 5) while keeping DONTNEED, an RT thread on a CPU delays that CPU's kworker and lengthens every drain (R6 e4).

### S1-4 — cold raw-copy pool allocates 16.6 MB zero-filled under `DngRecordingQueue::mutex_` (cause of first-take gaps: candidate; improvement: cheap)
- Claim: on a pool miss the copy worker runs `std::vector<uint8_t>(size)` (value-initialised: kernel zero-fills fresh pages on fault **and** libstdc++ memsets them) while holding `mutex_`; libcamera's thread blocks in `hasCapacityForFrame` for the duration (est. 2-8 ms), inside the serial per-frame cycle.
- Mechanism: `CC:4623-4636` (lock taken at :4625, allocation at :4636); pool ≤ 6 (`CC:5242-5245`), cleared on every (re)configure (`CC:8191`); writers free vectors beyond the cap (`CC:5144-5150`), so each queue excursion above ≈ 2 re-allocates on the way back up. Misses: the first ≈ 7 frames of the first take after boot or a mode change, then whenever more than ≈ 7 buffers circulate.
- Evidence for: code only; the 10-31 short takes were the first takes after a reboot (`HL:781`); the kb's "3 of 4 first takes after a (re)configure were lossy, later ones clean" (`working/clearhdr-design.md:119`). Against: the first gap came 2-4 s in, not in the first 7 frames; A0/A1's first frames were clean (one take each).
- Test: compare the first take after a mode change with the second, 10 pairs, WebUI closed; `perf trace -e syscalls:sys_enter_mmap,sys_enter_brk,sys_enter_munmap -p <pid>` and `perf stat -e page-faults -p <pid>` in the first 2 s; or a timed-wait log around `hasCapacityForFrame` (print when > 1 ms).
- Fix: preallocate `workers + queue + 2` buffers in `startAtFolder` (outside any lock), allocate with `posix_memalign`/a default-init allocator (no memset), never free inside a take, keep the pool across reconfigures when the frame size is unchanged. Expected: zero allocations after the first second; `hasCapacityForFrame` never waits more than µs. Risk: low (+ ≈ 100-200 MB resident).

### S1-5 — per-file fsync on exFAT = whole-device metadata flush + FLUSH per file (improvement)

Against: A0 = A1 at 25 fps on a trimmed NVMe (no measured harm); the per-file fsync exists for a reason (C0010: 0-byte files after a pull with only the folder fsync, `CC:4512-4516`).

### S1-7 — O_DIRECT cDNG (kb T24 patch, unbuilt) with the exFAT caveat (improvement, larger)
- Claim: direct I/O from a page-aligned raw copy removes the kernel copy and all page-cache work (no DONTNEED, no drain, no reclaim, no `balance_dirty_pages`): 3 copies → 1 at 16-bit.
- exFAT specifics (probable; kernel from memory, not re-read this session — settle with `git show v6.12:fs/exfat/inode.c | grep -n -A12 'exfat_direct_IO'`): `exfat_direct_IO` refuses a direct **write beyond `i_size_aligned`** and returns 0, which makes the VFS fall back to a buffered write silently — so a new file must first be extended (`ftruncate(fd, final_size)` → `exfat_cont_expand` allocates all 127 clusters in one `exfat_alloc_cluster(…, 127)` call, which also makes the per-cluster cost one call instead of 127); buffer, offset and length must be multiples of the logical block size (512 B; use 4 KiB) → IFD-first layout with a padded header, and the final partial block written buffered or padded (DNG permits padding).
- Evidence for: `HL:87-88` (`dd oflag=direct` cost no frames while buffered `dd` did); kb `working/patches/0001-cdng-direct-I-O-*.patch` exists. Against: the kb's synthetic "fallocate preallocation was worse" (buffered writes after preallocation — a different path, possibly zero-filling `[valid_size, pos)` per R6 a24); the kb's "not worth doing" rested on "neither stack ships it", not on a measurement (R3 §13).
- Test: port onto the copy worker, build on the Pi; `strace -f -e openat,fallocate,ftruncate,pwrite64,write -p` on one frame to prove no buffered fallback; A0 protocol; `perf stat` bus accesses; also removes the need for S1-2/S1-5's writeback machinery for DNG data.
- Expected: Core's copy traffic 2.49 → 0.83 GB/s at 25 fps; no page-cache growth; writes issued as ≈ 16 × 1 MiB bios per frame. Risk: medium (new I/O path; exFAT fallback; a USB bridge with FUA quirks).

### S1-10 — FAT-chained frames on fragmented free space (cause, post-trim regime; candidate)
- Claim: after one pass of the volume, `clu_srch_ptr` wraps and new frames fill deleted holes; a non-adjacent cluster converts the file to `ALLOC_FAT_CHAIN` and every later cluster costs two `exfat_ent_set` (FAT reads via synchronous `sb_bread` when cold, writes buffered). This is the `exfat_alloc_cluster > exfat_ent_set > __bread_gfp` stack of 2026-10-30. A trim restores the drive's write speed but does not defragment; a remount restarts the cursor at cluster 2 (oldest holes first).
- Evidence for: stack dumps (`HL:721-723`); kernel path (R2 §2.3, R4 §4d, R6 a10-a13, C-a2/C-a3); "one large file was clean" (`HL:722-723`). Against: A0/A1 clean after trim on a volume written ≈ 1 TB; whole 10-min clips deleted as blocks leave large contiguous holes, so chaining may be rare in practice.
- Test: `sudo filefrag -v /media/RAW/<late clip>/<frame>.dng` for 20 frames (extents > 1 = chained) vs a fresh format; `iostat -x 1 nvme0n1` during a take (`r/s > 0` = metadata misses); `perf top -p <core pid>` for `exfat_ent_set`/`__bread_gfp`.
- Expected: if chaining is common and reads appear, S1-11 and S1-14 become worth doing; if files are single-extent after a pass, close this. Risk: none.

### S1-12 — `discard` mount (assessment: do not; restates R4 §6 / R6 C-a5)
- 6.12 exFAT issues one **synchronous** discard per freed 128 KiB cluster inside `exfat_clear_bitmap`, under `bitmap_lock`: ≈ 1.9 million commands for a 15,000-frame delete, inside the deleting process, blocking any concurrent take's allocations. Core's idle FITRIM does the same work in one call (0.47 s measured). Test only if curious: `mount -o remount,discard` + `time rm -rf <clip>` vs `rm` + `time fstrim -v`.

### S1-14 — Host Memory Buffer for the DRAM-less drive (improvement, drive-side; the kb lists it as untested)
- Claim: the drive's **minimum** HMB is 64 MiB (`nvme: min host memory (64 MiB) above limit (0 MiB)`), so only `nvme.max_host_mem_size_mb=64` or more does anything (32 would still give none). - Caveat: on Pi 5 the HMB comes from CMA (no PCIe IOMMU); the rig's `CmaFree` was 32-496 kB, so 64 MiB needs `dtoverlay=vc4-kms-v3d,cma-256` (or similar) or the allocation fails ("failed to allocate host memory buffer") or starves display/camera allocations.
- Evidence: `HL:728`; R6 b1-b7 (code; PRs #6844, #7314). Against: sequential-write workloads gain little; the stall was cured by trim alone.
- Test: cmdline `nvme.max_host_mem_size_mb=64` + `cma-256`, reboot, `dmesg | grep -i "host memory"` (expect "allocated 64 MiB host memory buffer"), `grep Cma /proc/meminfo`; `fio --rw=randread --bs=4k --iodepth=1 --direct=1 --size=1G` latency before/after; then the wrap test of S1-10. Expected: random-read latency down; take-level effect only in the fragmented regime. Risk: CMA exhaustion if headroom is missing (camera/display allocation failures).

### S1-15 — USB SSD defaults (improvement)
- Claim: Apertar treats exFAT on usb-uas like pi-nvme (4 writers, queue 12, 1 s fsync, frames mode), while a USB 3.0 SSD on a Pi 5 cannot carry 415 MB/s and has an SLC-cache cliff; per-file fsync becomes a SCSI SYNCHRONIZE CACHE per file through the bridge.
- Proposal: per-class defaults in `MediaProfile`: usb-*: `SYNC_MS` 0 (keep the stop `syncfs`), ram buffer on with the 90 % safety stop (absorbs the cache cliff as a clean stop instead of per-frame drops), Log suggested by default in Clear HDR 16-bit, and a UI warning when the mode's MB/s exceeds the class envelope (Core already has a `dd conv=fdatasync` speed test, `APP:10341`; run it for ≥ 60 GB to pass the cache, or read the post-cache rate once per drive and store it).
- Test: `lsusb -t` (uas vs usb-storage), `fio --direct=1 --rw=write --bs=1M --size=100G` curve, `sdparm --get=WCE`, a 10-min Log-12 take and a 3-min linear take on the T7 with `drop[*]`, `peak_queue`, and the flush counts. Expected: linear 16-bit fails on any USB SSD; Log-12 marginal; the safety stop replaces drops. Risk: low.

### S1-16 — exFAT directory growth: O(N) create lookups under `s_lock` (assessment; candidate for long 50 fps takes)
- Claim: each frame-file create scans every used-or-deleted dentry of the clip directory (negative dentries are not trusted under `LOOKUP_CREATE`; the miss path rewinds to 0); with 5 dentries per file (33-char names) a 10-min 25 fps take ends at 75k dentries, a 10-min 50 fps take at 150k; at 100-200 ns per dentry that is ≈ 8-15 ms and ≈ 15-30 ms per create, holding `s_lock`, which the other writers take per 4 KiB block in `exfat_get_block`. O(N²) per take.
- Evidence for: `fs/exfat/dir.c@v6.12:exfat_find_dir_entry`, `namei.c:exfat_d_revalidate` (R6 a17-a18, C-a4); directory sizes computed. Against: A0's 15,000-file take was clean at 25 fps; the kb's synthetic sub-folder test (500-2500 files) "did not matter" (but the drive stall dominated those runs); the per-dentry cost is an estimate.
- Test: `strace -T -e trace=openat -p <writer tid>` late in a long take (open latency vs frame index); `perf top -p <core pid>` for `exfat_find_dir_entry` / `__find_get_block` in the last minutes.
- Mitigations (assessment): shorter frame names (≤ 15 chars after the clip prefix is impossible with the default template; 2 name dentries need ≤ 30 chars total — the configurable template `014a2bf` allows it); sub-folders break single-folder DNG-sequence import; a kernel directory index does not exist. Expected: matters only if the measured per-create time approaches the frame period at 50 fps. Risk: none (measurement).

### S1-17 — ram buffer (assessment; restates kb/R3 §5)
- `buffer=ram` gives ≈ 85 frames ≈ 3.4 s at 25 fps on the 4 GB board (1424 MiB measured budget) and a clean 90 % safety stop; it still drops when full and does nothing for capture gaps (the frames never reach the queue). cinepi-raw's permit is ≈ 2× larger (0.9 × MemAvailable, no floor) and blocks instead. Sensible default for pi-nvme and usb-* now that the stall class is understood: a worn, untrimmed drive then ends the take cleanly instead of losing 31 % of frames. Test: `APERTAR_MEDIA_TRIM=0`, write past one pass, compare frames vs ram.

### S1-18 — pre-roll, storage side (assessment; restates R3 L11 / R5 §6 / R7 §7 with the volumes)
- Unmeasured on both stacks; no stated mechanism from cinemate. Code-grounded: it warms the raw pool (only without a reconfigure) and exFAT metadata; it deletes 0.7-0.83 GB untrimmed (< 1 GiB) or triggers a whole-volume `fstrim` right before the take (≥ 1 GiB at 60 fps uncompressed), which `record_start` kills after 2 s; two fps changes blind the gap counter for 10-14 frames. Prior for a benefit on this rig: low (the measured stalls come minutes in; the first-seconds gaps appear 2-4 s after a take starts, which a warm pool could only help if S1-4 is real). Test: kb step 0b (COLD / WARM / SECOND, 6 pairs) + a "pre-roll then `fstrim`" arm; read the journal for `media: trim` and `storage preroll:` timestamps against the first `drop[capture-gap]`. Honest default until then: off, or on with trim-after-every-pre-roll.

## 11. Assessments (what is established / inferred / unknown)

**better_in_general.** Established: the one robust cause found by the campaign, the untrimmed drive's stall after ≈ 0.9 TB of writes (queue-full bursts, 18-77 % of files late, one controller hang), is cured by a trim (48 + 23 synthetic runs; one Core take each on an untrimmed drive, 31 % lost, and a trimmed one, 0 lost), and Core now trims at mount in 0.47 s and after ≥ 1 GiB WebUI deletes. Established: Core's write chain at 4 writers / queue 12 holds 415 MB/s for 10 minutes (A0, A1) and 525 MB/s for 60 s on exFAT over Gen 3 ×1. Inferred: the two writer-side changes of the drop-frames squash (Log: −25 % bytes, +1 copy pass on the copy worker; the `ram` buffer: a 3.4 s cushion with a safety stop) can only affect the queue-full class, which trim already removed; the write syscall sequence (DONTNEED, `sync_file_range`, 1 s fsync) is unchanged since 1.6.0 plus the 10-26 sync work. Unknown: no 3- or 10-minute take of either operator case exists on the `drop-frames` build (`d44b177` or later); its only takes are four short ones that lost 2-10 frames at capture with the WebUI open, one 3-min lossless take with no drop line, the pre-roll and the safety-stop take; the trim acceptance series (deletes past one volume pass without manual trims, vs `APERTAR_MEDIA_TRIM=0`) has not run; the touch-UI delete hole (S1-9) is untested.

**preroll_effect.** Established: nothing — no measurement on either stack, no stated mechanism from cinemate. Inferred from the code: possible warming of the raw copy pool and the exFAT metadata/dentry caches; definite side effects of 0.7-0.83 GB of freed untrimmed clusters per pre-roll below the 1 GiB trim threshold (or a whole-volume `fstrim` seconds before the take above it, killed by `record_start` after 2 s), and a blind window of the gap counter after its two fps changes. Unknown: everything the kb's COLD/WARM/SECOND protocol would answer; whether the 10-31 short-take build ran an automatic pre-roll and trim at boot minutes before those takes (`journalctl -u apertar-core -b | grep -E 'storage preroll|media: trim'`).

**clearhdr16_residual.** Established: 16.6 MB/frame at 25 fps (415 MB/s) is inside the drive and link envelope at Gen 3 ×1 (57 % of the direct-write rate; 10 min clean twice on a trimmed drive), and Log brings it to 12.45 MB (311 MB/s); every residual loss in this case is a capture gap with the queue at 1-2 of 12 (A2 one run; four short takes with the WebUI open; C0001's 12 gaps under the old 1 s `syncfs`). Inferred: the storage-side mechanisms with a route to the capture thread are the per-frame DONTNEED's global LRU drain (25/s), the 26 device flushes per second of the sync thread, the cold raw-pool allocation under the queue mutex in a take's first frames, a trim or pre-roll finishing shortly before, and the extra copy pass's DRAM traffic — none measured, all cheap to test; the kb's A2 reading ("kernel writeback alone disturbs the CFE") does not hold as a mechanism because A2 still initiated per-frame writeback. The WebUI's downsample on the completion thread (not storage) is the time-correlated candidate. Unknown: which of these, if any, produces the first-seconds gaps; a repeat of A2; any long take with Log on; whether the drive's firmware (E27T R2) and link stay at 8 GT/s through a 10-minute take.

**sdr50_residual.** Established: in the only measurement (T34 M3, 3840×2076, 60 s ×2, untrimmed drive at 78-82 %) storage kept up at the 525 MB/s delivered (queue ≤ 3/12, 0 queue-full, 0 write failures) and 12 % of frames never left the CFE as steady one-frame skips, unchanged with 8 raw buffers; Log cannot apply (COMP1 stream). Inferred: the loss is a serial-cycle deficit in libcamera's pipeline (back end with TDN at ≈ 29 MB/frame against a 20 ms budget; R8/R9), so no storage change moves it; if that is fixed, uncompressed 4K 50 fps (622 MB/s at 2160) sits at 85 % of the measured direct-write ceiling of this link and above anything Core has sustained, 60 fps (746) exceeds it, and the fsync/drain counts and the 30,000-file directory double the exFAT-side costs — 10-bit (518 MB/s) or lossless (300-455 MB/s, CPU-tight) are the storage-side ways to fit. Unknown: the drive's post-pSLC sustained rate on this link after a trim; a counted take at 3840×2160 on a trimmed drive with the WebUI closed and `dropped_copy_slot` read; whether cinemate ever recorded this case anywhere (no record exists; its own SDR path would move 2× the CFE bytes).
