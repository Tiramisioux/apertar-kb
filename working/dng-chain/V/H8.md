### 1.5 DRAM traffic — where H8's arithmetic goes wrong

H8 and R3:80-85 / S1:188 count each pass as 16.6 MB read + 16.6 MB write **through DRAM**: 3 × 33.2 = 99.5 MB (Core) vs 2 × 33.2 = 66.4 MB (cinepi-raw). R8 adopted the opposite convention one report over: "the writer's scratch stays in L2 (491 KiB / 369 KiB) and is not counted" (R8:76), and R8:12 notes the chunk sizes "all fit the 512 KiB L2". The two reports are inconsistent and M picked the larger number.

What actually reaches DRAM (per core L2 512 KiB, shared L3 2 MB, confirmed from `bcm2712.adoc`):

| Pass | Core, 16-bit linear | cinepi-raw, 16-bit linear |
| --- | --- | --- |
| read the camera buffer | 16.6 r (copy worker) | 16.6 r (encode worker) |
| write the intermediate | 16.6 w (raw copy; 16.6 MB > L3, so it is written back) | 16.6 w (`mem_buf`, likewise) |
| read the intermediate | 16.6 r (writer; written by another core) | 16.6 r (kernel `copy_from_user` from `mem_buf`) |
| scratch write + read | 480 KiB buffer, rewritten 34 times by the same thread and read back by the kernel on the same core within microseconds: **0 to 33.2** (0 if L2/L3-resident, 33.2 if every line round-trips) | — |
| write the page cache | 16.6 w | 16.6 w |
| **total** | **66.4 + (0 … 33.2) MB** | **66.4 MB** |

The extra Core pass is one more 16.6 MB memcpy of **CPU time** on a writer thread (~2.8 ms at the measured ~6 GB/s single-core memcpy rate, §3.1) and cache bandwidth; its DRAM cost is between nothing and 33 MB. Two things make the low end likely: the scratch (480 KiB) fits the writer core's L2 (512 KiB) and certainly the 2 MB L3, and the producer and consumer of each chunk are the same thread on the same core within one `write()` call. One thing pulls the other way: Cortex-A76 write-streaming mode (CPUECTLR_EL1; the A76 erratum 1220197 workaround is "disable write streaming to the L2") stops allocating in L1/L2 after a run of full-line write misses; if the per-row 7,680 B memcpys trip it, the scratch writes go to L3 (still not DRAM). Status: **probable**, and directly measurable (`perf stat -e bus_access -t <writer tid>`; see §4).

So the headline "99.5 vs 66 MB of memory traffic per frame (2.5 vs 1.7 GB/s at 25 fps)" is the **upper bound** of a 0 to +0.83 GB/s difference and probably close to the lower end for the linear 16-bit case. The CPU-pass count (3 vs 2) is right; the DRAM implication is overstated.

A memcpy at 6 GB/s moves 12 GB/s through DRAM (read + write) — so one A76 core streaming can take ~70 % of the theoretical interface by itself; two streaming cores saturate it. That is the quantitative basis for "CPU copies contend with the back end", and it is sound.

### 3.2 Is the route from Core's copies to the back end plausible?

- Recording as a whole: 4.9 GB/s at 25 fps against 17 GB/s peak (29 %) or 10-12 GB/s practical (40-50 %). At that utilisation LPDDR4X queueing raises latency for latency-bound consumers; the measured 2.6× back-end slowdown and 1.44× cycles/frame on the CameraManager thread (HL:188, 161-162) are what that looks like. **Plausible and measured at the recording level.**
- Core's *copies* (the term H8 names): copy worker + writers ≈ 1.66 GB/s of DRAM traffic at 25 fps (§1.5), the same as cinepi-raw's chain would cost on the same rig; the *removable* difference is 0 to 0.83 GB/s (linear) or ≈ 1.0 GB/s (Log). Removing 0-0.8 GB/s from a 4.9 GB/s total is a 0-17 % cut in the load that stretches the back end — a few hundred µs of a 36 ms cycle at most if the slowdown scaled linearly, which it does not (queueing is convex, so the effect could be larger near saturation or nil far from it). **Unmeasured; small is the prior.**
- The dd evidence lies at 3-4× Core's traffic and carries allocation/reclaim confounds, so it bounds nothing at Core's scale.
- Burstiness: Core's writer memcpys are 4 threads × ~3 ms per frame, the copy worker ~3 ms + sync; they are not synchronised with the back end's ~13-35 ms window. q3's "bursts of 15-20 GB/s while the back end runs" (q3:20-21) is an estimate, not a trace.

Conclusion for lens 3: the mechanism (DRAM/interconnect contention slowing the PiSP back end and the CameraManager thread) is real and the best-supported route in the kb; H8's attribution of a meaningful share of it to Core's extra copy pass is not quantitatively supported, because the pass's DRAM cost is probably near zero for the linear 16-bit case and ≈ 1 GB/s only with Log.

## 4. Corrections and a better test

### 4.1 Corrections to H8's wording

1. "~99.5 MB vs 66 MB of memory traffic per frame (2.5 vs 1.7 GB/s at 25 fps)" → "three CPU copy passes vs two; DRAM traffic ≈ 66 MB/frame (1.66 GB/s at 25 fps) in **both** chains for linear 16-bit, because Core's 480 KiB scratch is L2/L3-resident (upper bound +33 MB/frame if it is not; unmeasured). With Log, Core ≈ 95 MB vs cinepi-raw ≈ 54 MB (+1.0 GB/s at 25 fps): the in-place LUT pass over the 16.6 MB copy is real DRAM traffic. At 4K50 SDR 12-bit the sign reverses: Core ≈ 37 MB vs cinepi-raw ≈ 53 MB plus double the CFE DMA (COMP1 vs unpacked)."
2. "~43 syscalls per DNG against 4" → "43 on the writer thread (40 + `startWriteback`'s 3); ≈ 46-48 per frame with the sync thread's fsync and the copy worker's two dma-buf sync ioctls; cinepi-raw 4 plus one stdout line. The count itself is not a cost (< 0.1 ms/frame); `fadvise(DONTNEED)` and `sync_file_range` are the two that do work (H3)."
3. "The kb measured libcamera's thread losing IPC (1.87 → 1.30) while recording" → add "with the whole recording load on (copy worker, four writers, page-cache copy, NVMe DMA, per-frame writeback IPIs); nothing isolates the third copy".
4. "buffered dd costs frames while dd oflag=direct does not" → add "at 556 vs 733 MB/s with free memory falling 923 → 157 MB in the buffered run; the tmpfs loop at ~2 GB/s (≈ 6 GB/s of DRAM traffic) lost 11 frames in 6 s; n = 1 each, preview only (T22:16-18)".
5. Writers on exFAT = 4: confirmed; note the stale comments MP:144-146 and CC:4118 that say 2.
6. The copy worker's memcpy is 16,588,800 B (active rows), not 16.9 MB (CC:8894-8901).
7. cinepi-raw's "one row pass" also runs the ClipCeiling sampler on the first frame of a take only (DE:1171-1204) and a colour-JPEG thumbnail per frame from the lores buffer (cinemate default mode 3, `config_loader.py:379`): CPU work Core does not do; unmeasured.
8. R3:80-85, S1:188, S2:272 (99.5 / 120 / 133 MB) and R8:76 (scratch not counted) disagree; the final page should use R8's convention with the upper bound stated.
9. A0 bounds the effect: the 3-copy chain ran 10 minutes at 25 fps with 0 lost frames; H8 is a candidate only for the thin-margin cases (30 fps Clear HDR, Log on the copy thread).

### 4.2 Better test (in order of cost)

1. **No code change — is the scratch DRAM traffic at all?** During a 60 s 4K25 16-bit take: `perf stat -e bus_access,l3d_cache_refill,cycles,instructions -t <one writer tid>` (writer tids from `top -H`). Expected per frame per writer (1/4 of the frames): bus traffic ≈ 16.6 MB read + 16.6 MB page-cache write → the scratch is cache-resident and the "+33 MB" is wrong; ≈ 50-66 MB → it round-trips DRAM and the upper bound holds.
2. **No code change — the fps-vs-traffic curve.** Preview only at 30 fps Clear HDR 16-bit (the steadily failing case): run a user-space copy load pinned to one core at controlled rates (e.g. `stress-ng --stream 1 --taskset 3` throttled, or a 32 MB memcpy loop with `nanosleep` tuned to 0.5 / 1 / 2 / 4 GB/s), read delivered fps and `hwcount.py` cycles/frame on the CameraManager thread. This replaces the dd runs (no page allocation, no reclaim, no NVMe) and places Core's 0-1.0 GB/s delta on a measured curve.
3. **One-function A/B.** In `writeRawPayload` (CE:1862-1910), for `frame.packing == Unpacked && frame.bitDepth == Bits16 && frame.stride == layout.rowBytes`, call `writeAll(fd, frame.data, layout.byteCount)` (or 64-row slices from `frame.data`) and skip the scratch. Protocol: 3 × 60 s takes each build at **30 fps** Clear HDR 16-bit uncompressed, WebUI closed, drive trimmed; read delivered fps, `dropped_gap_estimate`, and `hwcount.py` on the CameraManager thread (cycles/frame, IPC). Run at 30, not 25: A0 shows 25 fps is already clean and cannot show a gain.
4. **Log.** Fold the LUT into the 12-bit packer (read the 16-bit copy, table, write packed 12 — what cinepi-raw does at DE:1226-1263) and drop `applyCdngLogFrame` from `copyLoop`; same protocol at 25 fps with Log on, plus the copy thread's per-frame time (a timing line around CC:4806-4821 or `perf trace -p` on the copy thread). This is the case where §1.6 gives a real +1.0 GB/s and the copy thread is serial.

## 5. Observations vs conclusions

Observations (confirmed from source or logs): copy passes 3/4 vs 2; chunk 491,520 B = 64 rows; 34 payload writes, 36 `write()` per DNG; 43 writer-thread syscalls (≈ 48 per frame) vs 4 (+1); 4 writers on exFAT since 0358fcf; memcpy size 16,588,800 B; Core's Log LUT in place on the copy thread; cinepi-raw folds LUT and pack into one row pass and writes 16-bit verbatim for a 16-bit sensor mode; cinemate asks unpacked raw on the Pi 5 and a JPEG thumbnail per frame; IPC 1.87 → 1.30 with unchanged instructions and L3 refills; dd rows as in T22:16-18; A0/A1 clean with the 3-copy chain; LPDDR4X 32-bit 17 GB/s, L2 512 KiB, L3 2 MB, single-core memcpy ≈ 6 GB/s.

Conclusions (inferred): the scratch pass is cache-resident, so the DRAM-traffic difference for linear 16-bit is near zero (probable; test 1 settles it); with Log the difference is ≈ +1.0 GB/s (arithmetic); at 4K50 SDR the sign reverses (arithmetic from the formats each stack requests); DRAM contention is real at the recording level but the removable share attributable to H8 is small and unmeasured; H8 is **plausible** as a description of the chains and as a low-cost improvement lead, **not** established as a cause of the residual drops, and its headline DRAM numbers should be replaced by the bounds above.
