# V/H1 — 4K 50 fps SDR frames are lost upstream of Core's writers (libcamera's serial per-frame cycle), not in storage

Verifier for H1 (M.md). Trees read: Apertar-Core `92d0970` (`CC` = `src/camera/CameraController.cpp`, `MP` = `src/util/MediaProfile.hpp`, `SP` = `src/util/StoragePreroll.hpp`) and `4d5fdd1` (the T34 M3 build, via `git show`); upstream libcamera `c682979` at `/home/user/raspberrypi/libcamera` (`LC`); Tiramisioux/libcamera `apertar` `6b0b49f` and `cinemate` `bfe0b76` (shallow clones in `../fork-libcamera/apertar`, `LA` / `LCM`); kb (`HL` = `apertar-kb/lessons/hardware-log.md`, working tree, no commit); cinemate-handbook (`HB` = `lessons/hardware-log.md`, kb cites `c99ff77`); cinemate `4990afc` (`src/module/rp1_regime.py`); cinepi-raw `63a08d5` (`core/options.cpp`); kernel rpi-6.12.y `rp1_cfe/cfe.c`, `clk-rp1.c`, `rp1.dtsi` (WebFetch summaries, quoted lines only); will127534/imx585-v4l2-driver README (WebFetch). Labels: **confirmed** = read in source or in a logged number; **probable** = derived, reasoning given; **unconfirmed** = needs the Pi.

## 0. Verdict

**Plausible, not established.** Every counted number in H1 holds, the libcamera and kernel mechanism is real and reads exactly as H1 says, and the R6 reading (upstream's 390 MPix/s cap clamping 4K to ~44 fps) is **refuted for this rig** by four independent facts (§2.5, §3.2). But the load-bearing quantity — a serial cycle of ~22.7 ms at 4K SDR 12-bit while recording — was never measured, and the only later 4K SDR 12-bit measurement at a higher rate (the 2026-11-01 pre-roll, 60 fps, 115 frames at a measured 60.0 fps with no skip) bounds the mean cycle at **≤ 17.1 ms** on `d44b177` (§3.3), which contradicts R9 §5's 19-23 ms estimate for the same back-end bytes. So the loss mechanism named by H1 (CFE buffer starvation behind a serial cycle longer than the period) is the best reading of T34 M3; the attribution of that cycle to the PiSP back end (and hence to TDN) is a model with one measurement against it; and whether the drop-frames build (`92d0970`) still shows the M3 loss at all is unknown. One correction changes a fact in H1: the M3 baseline had **4** raw buffers (plus libcamera's 2 internal CFE buffers), not 10 (§2.4).

### 2.2 The CFE skip (kernel rpi-6.12.y `drivers/media/platform/raspberrypi/rp1_cfe/cfe.c`)

Quoted from the file (WebFetch): `cfe_check_job_ready` loops over enabled nodes and `if (list_empty(&node->dma_queue)) { cfe_dbg_verbose("%s: [%s] has no buffer, unable to schedule job\n", ...); return false; }`; `cfe_sof_isr_handler`: `node->cur_frm = node->next_frm; node->next_frm = NULL; node->fs_count++;`. `cfe_queue_setup` forces >= 3 buffers, `q->min_queued_buffers = 1`. So a frame for which no job could be prepared is not written anywhere and the frame-start counter still advances — the app sees a hole in `SensorTimestamp`, no error. **Confirmed.** This is the one mechanism that produces exactly the logged signature (single holes, period multiples).

### 2.3 Core's capture-gap detector (`CC:9475-9528@92d0970`; same logic at `4d5fdd1`)

- Input: `SensorTimestamp` of the request (fallback: buffer timestamp) and `FrameDuration` metadata normalised to us (`CC:8904-8916`; `normalizeFrameDurationToUs` `CC:3604-3629` only resolves the ns/us ambiguity, it does not snap to the requested fps).
- Rule: `delta > 1.5 x nominal` -> `estimate = round(delta/nominal) - 1`, added to `recordingGapEstimate_`; a line `drop[capture-gap]: +N frame(s) estimate dt_us=... nominal_us=... total_estimate=...` on the first gap, every 30th accumulated frame, or when `N > 1` (`CC:9511-9525`). `dropped_gap_estimate=` is printed at stop (`CC:7355`).
- Consequence for the R6/R9 question: a sensor **clamped** to a 21.7-22.7 ms period runs **uniformly** at that period and reports it as `FrameDuration`; `delta/nominal = 1.0`, so the detector logs **nothing** and `dropped_gap_estimate` stays 0, while the DNG count still falls 12 % short of `fps x seconds`. M3 logged `dropped_gap_estimate=355` = the whole shortfall, 13 lines, `dt` 40 ms (two 20 ms periods). 1 + floor(355/30) = 12 lines; the 13th is one gap with estimate > 1 or rounding of the count. **Confirmed from code + log: the sensor ran at 20 ms and the CFE skipped frames; the pattern is incompatible with a pixel-rate clamp.**

### 3.3 The pre-roll measurement (2026-11-01, `d44b177`) and what it bounds

`storage_preroll_last = ok: 2.0 s at 60 fps (measured 60.0), 115 frames, 0.7 GB` (`HL:842-855`; kb `drop-frames/README.md:111-113`). Code: `measuredFps = (frames - 1) x 1e6 / (last - first)` over the **sensor timestamps** of the first and last frame the take **enqueued** (`SP:200-205`; `CC:8974-8979, 11530-11535@92d0970`), counted only after `waitForFramePeriod` saw the new period (`CC:7435-7455`). 114 intervals at exactly 16.67 ms = 1.900 s; one single skipped frame would read 114/1.9167 = 59.5, printed `%.1f`. **So the 60.0 means zero capture gaps across 115 consecutive frames at 3840x2160 12-bit, 60 fps, lossless cDNG (5 encoder workers), with 4 + 2 CFE buffers.** The "115 of 120" that R9 §5 read as a possible loss is the take's start/stop edges (1.9 s of frames in a 2.0 s take), not skips. Correction to R9.

Bound on the cycle: with 3 buffers of slack (6 - 2 at the CFE - 1 in the pipeline) the backlog can absorb 3 x 16.67 = 50 ms over 114 frames -> mean cycle <= 16.67 + 0.44 = **17.1 ms** (probable; the slack count is +-1). The BE bytes per frame in that mode are the same as in uncompressed SDR (the BE reads the COMP1 CFE buffer either way; R9 §5 row 1: 29.0 MB with TDN) and larger than in the M3 crop (27.8 MB). R9 §5 predicted <= 17.4 ms BE + 2-5 ms software = 19-23 ms "while recording" for this case and called M3's 22.7 ms "exactly" that. The pre-roll says the whole cycle, software included, averaged <= 17.1 ms while five lossless workers ran. Either (a) the Pi's `imx585.json` has no `tdn` (then BE ~10.6 MB and the TDN lever of H2/S2-1 is moot for SDR), or (b) the "x2.6 recording stretch" taken from the 16-bit case does not transfer to COMP1 SDR (fewer CPU passes competing for DRAM), or (c) M3's extra ~5 ms came from something not present in the pre-roll (a WebUI MJPEG client on the completion thread, `CC:9347-9444`; the 4d5fdd1 build; the crop mode; the untrimmed drive's writers behaving differently). All three are open. **This is the main evidence tension with H1 as worded.** It does not refute the generic "serial cycle > period" reading of M3; it refutes "the BE-bound cycle is ~22.7 ms at 4K SDR" as an established number.

## 4. Mechanism and domain

### 4.1 The RP1 front-end limit and whether the overclock is in effect in hardware

- The limit is real and documented in libcamera's own comment: CSI2 -> ISP-FE at 400 MPix/s with a 64 kbit FIFO, so the constraint acts per scanline and horizontal blanking can absorb it (`LC controller.cpp:62-77`). The fork's comment and `rp1_regime.py` give the model: 2 px per RP1 `clk_sys` cycle, 200 MHz stock (`LA controller.cpp:30-38`; `rp1_regime.py:3-5, 70-72` "Measured on a CM5: stock 200 MHz -> 380 MPix/s, overlay -> 580"). The imx585 README: "by default RPI5/RP1 has a limit of 400Mpix/s processing speed, without overclocking RP1 (hence the Camera Frontend) you will be limited to ~43.8 FPS @ 4K" (WebFetch, verbatim). **Confirmed as the vendor model; the 2 px/clk figure itself is unconfirmed (no datasheet reachable).**
- Arithmetic at HMAX 550 (7.407 us lines, 3840 px, 12-bit over 4 x 1782 Mbps): the line's data arrives in 6.46 us (594 MPix/s burst). At a 400 MPix/s drain the FIFO gains 3840 - 400 x 6.46 = 1256 px per burst and loses 400 x 0.95 = 380 px in the gap: **+876 px (10.5 kbit) per line, overflow of the 64 kbit FIFO after ~6 lines, every frame.** The Pi records clean 4K pictures at HMAX 550 in every 4K mode (films shot 10-27/10-28; L1/L2 pixel checks `HL:212`), and the probe shows the lines are not stretched (§2.5). Hence the FE drains >= 518 MPix/s -> `clk_sys` >= ~270 MHz -> the `rp1-clk-333mhz` overlay is in effect in hardware. **Probable** (rests on the 2 px/clk model).
- Kernel: `clk-rp1.c` gives `clk_sys` `.max_freq = 200 * MHz` and `rp1_clock_choose_div_and_prate` rejects rates above it ("Prevent overclocks"); the handbook nevertheless read `clk_sys` 333333333 in `clk_summary` with the overlay (`HB:293,306`) and later "~333 MHz in both regimes" on 6.12.93 (`HB:855-857, 3160-3162`), which is why cinemate stopped trusting the clock reading and keys the cap on config.txt's overlay line (`rp1_regime.py:28-55`). How the overlay gets past `max_freq` (PLL_SYS raised with the `clk_sys` divider left at 1, so the rate follows the parent without a `clk_sys` set_rate) is **unconfirmed**; it does not bear on H1 beyond the point above.
- The `rp1-clk-333mhz` overlay is not in the upstream overlays README (two thirds read; the last third not fetched); it is the generated overlay of will127534/rp1-overclock-tool (kb `q6-link.md:268,555`). Confirmed (kb).

### 4.2 Why "22.7 ms" is a model, not a measurement

R9 §5 scales one measured point (Clear HDR 16-bit: BE 13.4 ms idle, 34.6 ms "start -> done" while recording, which the kb itself notes is a log interval, not hardware time, `research-30fps-answer.md:125-127`) by bytes to 4K SDR COMP1. Two assumptions carry it: that the BE is memory-bound (idle 57.6 MB / 13.4 ms = 4.3 GB/s), and that recording stretches it x2.6 regardless of the CPU-side traffic (the 16-bit case has two extra 33.8 MB CPU passes per frame on the same thread). The number that would settle it (`be_cycle.py` S2 at 50/60 fps) has not been taken; kb `ramp-mode-ladder.md:48-50, 70-73` asked for it on 10-28 and it was never run (R5 C11).
