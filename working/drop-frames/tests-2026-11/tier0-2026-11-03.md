## 2. What lengthens the BE job (measured)

- **Two CPU passes per HDR16 frame run on libcamera's CameraManager thread when the frame arrives**, not when the pipeline is
  idle: the Image dequeue handler takes 8.1-10.4 ms (Image to Config dequeue of the same frame; 0.05 ms in SDR12 in the same
  logs: probable that it is the fork's in-place 16-bit swap, 33.8 MB read + write, found in the code on 10-26), then RawStats
  6.2-7.3 ms. In a saturated take both run during the BE job of an earlier frame (`frm-P1.log` around 1:38:22.42: one 60 ms
  BE job overlaps two swaps and two RawStats).
- **The BE job is 2-3 times longer when it overlaps another frame's swap** (median S2 in ms, pooled over the 8 takes, n in
  brackets; slack = time from the previous frame's ISP complete, where Core's and the UI's work on that frame starts):

  | Swap overlap of the job | slack 0-2 ms (queued) | slack 2-16 ms | slack > 16 ms (pipeline was idle) |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | 0-0.5 ms | 20.4 [3,490] | 18.2 [268] | **13.2** [7,084] |
  | 0.5-3 ms | 25.9 [298] | 25.1 [17] | 23.2 [153] |
  | 3-6 ms | 28.9 [477] | 27.2 [38] | 25.9 [170] |
  | > 6 ms | **40.1** [18,479] | 39.4 [1,595] | 32.9 [1,333] |

  Every take shows the same gradient (per-take tables in script section 2). Part of it is mechanical (a long job catches
  more of the next swap); with the exposure fixed at the job's start instead (a swap already running when the job starts,
  against no swap for its first 15 ms) the job is still 5.2 ms longer when queued (42.1 against 36.9 ms, n = 934 / 14,184)
  and 10.1 ms when idle (n = 8 only). So the swap and RawStats slow the BE (measured); by how much per job lies between
  ~5 and ~20 ms (probable).
- **Starting right after the previous frame completed adds ~7 ms** at equal swap overlap (13.2 against 20.4 ms, 32.9
  against 40.1 ms): the work Core (copy, writers) and the UI (preview) start on the completed frame. The two cannot be
  separated with these logs (no UI threads).
- **Every CPU pass costs more per frame in the losing takes** (script section 6): Core's recording threads 30.3 ms of CPU per
  delivered frame in the clean pv0b-P1, 41.8-45.1 ms in the two worst (+38-49 %); CameraManager 16.3 against 19.9-20.0 ms
  (+23 %). The CameraManager thread's CPU rises 3.7 ms per frame while S2 rises 32 ms, so the S2 growth is not work on that
  thread; it is BE hardware time under memory contention (probable, the same reading as the dd run, which slows the BE
  ninefold with CPU memory traffic alone).

## 3. The two states (probable mechanism, from measured timings)

With the pipeline idle, a frame's BE job starts 16.4-18.0 ms after its CFE dequeue (swap + RawStats + IPA), so **the next
frame arrives, and its swap and RawStats begin, 22-24 ms into the job** (script section 4):

| Take | Lost | Jobs >= that time | Jobs that found a queue |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| pv0b-P1 | 0 | 0.11 | 0.12 |
| pv0b-P2 | 0 | 0.54 | 0.54 |
| g2-P1 | 2 | 0.56 | 0.52 |
| pv12-P1 | 4 | 0.75 | 0.73 |
| g2-P2, frm-P2, pv12-P2, frm-P1 | 108, 181, 330, 590 | 0.95-0.97 | 0.88-0.91 |

A job that ends within ~23 ms runs alone (10-17 ms). One that runs past it gets the next frame's swap and RawStats (~17 ms
of CPU streaming) on top and lengthens further; the next frame then waits, its job starts at once after the previous one
ends, on top of Core's work on the completed frame, and overlaps the following swap in turn. That loop holds S2 at 38-43 ms
(cycle 40-45 ms) until all 8 raw buffers are held and the CFE drops frames, which is the saturated state of findings
section 1 (stays there, starts within the first 20-40 s). **The threshold that matters is ~23 ms per BE job, not 40 ms**: a
take stays stable while its jobs, under the take's background traffic, mostly end before the next frame's swap begins.
At 30 fps (`31d5418`, Clear HDR 16-bit to the driver maximum) the frame period is 33.3 ms and the same arithmetic puts the
threshold near 16 ms: HDR16 at 30 needs BE jobs near their isolated time, under recording traffic.

Prediction (testable on one unit): a change that keeps BE jobs under ~23 ms during a take (fewer BE bytes) or keeps the swap
and RawStats off the BE job shows a queued share near pv0b-P1's 0.12 and no losses; a change that adds background traffic
raises the share of jobs past the threshold first.

## 4. The 1 Hz counters do not track S2 inside a take (script section 5)

Spearman rho of the per-second median S2 inside each take: kswapd reclaim -0.25..0.12, compaction -0.15..0.20, PSI some
-0.30..0.17, watermark boost -0.12..0.16, Dirty -0.11..0.11, Writeback -0.17..0.16 (all 8 takes). The pooled values
(reclaim 0.51, PSI 0.48) come from between-take differences: the two worst takes were the ones that reclaimed heavily.
Within takes, the CPU time of Core's recording threads goes with S2 (0.13-0.61, pooled 0.54), NVMe writes weakly in the
stable takes (0.22-0.39). Reclaim stays a co-factor of the worst takes, not the per-frame driver (measured).
