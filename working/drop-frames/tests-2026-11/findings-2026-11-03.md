## 1. The mechanism of a losing take (measured)

- **Where**: libcamera's ISP back end. With libcamera's RPi debug log (`LIBCAMERA_LOG_LEVELS=IPARPI:ERROR,RPI:0,RPiStream:0`,
  `LIBCAMERA_LOG_FILE` in `/run/apertar-media`: through journald ~half the lines were suppressed), `libcam_stages.py`
  follows each raw buffer. The CFE delivers every 40 ms in every take and libcamera's own stats stay at ~6.5 ms; the
  back end (`Input re-queue to ISP` -> `ISP Input buffer complete`) takes ~10-11 ms with the preview alone and 20-44 ms
  while recording. A take loses frames once the back end reaches ~40 ms: libcamera runs one frame at a time through it,
  frames queue in front of the IPA step (~200-260 ms, 5-6 frames), the 8 raw buffers are all held and the CFE has none
  for the next frame (8 of 8 takes on 11-02: lost = `owned_by_drv = 0` completions).
- **Two states**: a take is near capacity but stable (back end 21-37 ms, wait ~17 ms) or saturated from its first
  20-40 s on (back end 39-44 ms, wait 200-260 ms) and stays there; which one is not fixed by the take's position.
- **Memory contention alone produces it**: preview only, not recording, take mode: two `dd if=/dev/zero of=/dev/null
  bs=64M` for 60 s raise the back end from 10.9 to 95 ms (p90 148), the wait to 605 ms and the delivery interval to 92 ms
  (more than half the frames lost), and everything returns to idle levels at once after it. In the SDR12 idle mode the
  same load doubles the back end (23.7 ms) without loss.

## 2. What was tried and did not cure it (measured, n = 1 unit each)

| Change | P1 / P2 lost | Back end | Reading |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 16 raw buffers (`apertar-tune set raw_buffers=16`) | 26 / 123 | user space holds ~11.5 of 16 | more buffers fill up; losses without `owned_by_drv = 0` (a second loss path behind the CFE) |
| G2 reference (8 buffers, full preview) | 2 / 108 | 21-37 / 39 ms | the losing take is the saturated one |
| UI preview off during takes (`APERTAR_UI_PREVIEW_RECORDING_FPS=0`) | 0 / 0 | 9-13 / 21-35 ms | clean; but see the next row |
| UI preview 12 fps during takes | 4 / 330 | 35-38 / 41-43 ms | the dose-response is not monotone: the UI preview is not the lever under sync (the preview-off unit, ~1 in 4 by chance) |
| `[storage] buffer=frames` (A0's mode) | 590 / 181 | 43-44 / 40 ms | the RAM queue's footprint is not the lever |

The two worst takes (330 and 590 lost) were also the ones with heavy kswapd reclaim under sync (723 k and 849 k
pages) and the lowest CameraManager IPC (1.29, 1.31): memory pressure goes with the slowest back end.

## 3. Method and setup findings

- `/dev/shm` is not safe for files of user pi: logind's `RemoveIPC=yes` deletes them when pi's last login session ends
  (the first 16-buffer override vanished that way). Overrides, take logs and traces now live in `/run/apertar-media`
  (Core's `config/apertar-media.tmpfiles.conf`, installed 2026-11-03; it also gives the eject markers their directory).
- The card in the CFexpress hat was swapped twice while mounted during a unit's cool-down (20:52, 20:58); the unit
  loop now checks the card's serial and `warm.bin` before P1 and P2. User's card: Crucial P310 serial 25184FF10656.
- `b1-arm.sh` run twice, or a stray trace file, blocks the unit loop until the file is gone.
- The take script resets the compression to `none` before each take and restores only the ladder switch and fps;
  User's ladder rung 2 now reads SDR12 <= 50 uncompressed (11-02: <= 30 lossless JPEG): whether a script or User changed
  it is open; rung 1 (HDR16 lossless JPEG <= 25) encodes at ~17 fps (pre-roll `estimated_capacity_fps=16.84`).

## 4. What it means for the targets, and next

Target 1 (HDR16 25 fps, 0 lost, live preview) fails at the ISP back end's per-frame time under the memory traffic of
a take, not at the drive, the CSI-2 receiver or the UI's drawing alone. The levers are the take's memory traffic and
the back end's work per frame. Candidates, cheapest first (to be planned with User, outside the original plan):
1. Measure the take's memory traffic and which part of the write path carries it: per-take bandwidth from the PMU
   (`bus_access` is CameraManager's only) or a memory-bandwidth counter for the whole SoC; then remove one source at a
   time (the per-frame raw copy, the buffered DNG `write`, the page cache) and watch the back end.
2. Direct I/O for the DNG writes (`O_DIRECT` from the raw pool: no page-cache copy, no DONTNEED, no reclaim; the
   media profile already has an unused `directIo` flag; T24, not carried in this fork).
3. Less back-end work per frame while recording (a smaller or less frequent ISP preview output), a libcamera-side
   change.
B3's sysctl cells keep their plan but are unlikely to matter (the knobs act on reclaim, which is a co-factor here).
