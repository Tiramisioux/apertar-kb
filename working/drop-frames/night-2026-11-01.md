## 1. The result in one paragraph

The target was 4K Clear HDR 16-bit uncompressed cDNG at 25 fps with **no** lost frames while the touch UI shows the live
picture (peaking and zebra on). The losses were single frames missing at capture (Core's `dropped_gap_estimate`; the
write queue never filled). Most of the evening was spent on the touch UI's GPU work, because stopping or freezing the UI
removed the losses. After a reboot the same setup was clean once and then drifted again, and the drift followed **page-
cache pressure**: Core's DNG writer called `posix_fadvise(DONTNEED)` right after each write, on still-dirty pages, which
frees nothing and drains the per-CPU page lists on every core per frame, while the page cache filled and `kswapd`
reclaimed ~13 GB per 3-minute take. Setting `APERTAR_DNG_DONTNEED=sync` (drop the pages after the once-a-second `fsync`)
took the 3-minute takes to 0 lost and a 10-minute take to 25 of 15,023 (0.17 %, 18 of them in the last minute with the
drive 97 % full); with CineMate's dirty-page limits added (`vm.dirty_bytes` 1 GiB, `dirty_background_bytes` 256 MiB,
`min_free_kbytes` 64 MiB) a 10-minute take on the empty drive lost **0 of 15,025** and kswapd reclaimed nothing (#80). The UI was the last straw on an overloaded memory system, not the root cause (probable). Upstream
Apertar has the same per-frame `DONTNEED` call hard-coded (KB `lessons/upstream-issues.md` #44).

`drop-frames` @ `adac040`, UI `drop-frames` @ `c13b846`, and the kernel 6.12 memory-management source

### 8.1 Verdict

The **fix is right; the stated mechanism is half right; the proof is not finished.**

| Part of the claim | Status | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `writer` mode's `posix_fadvise(DONTNEED)` on a just-written DNG "frees nothing" | **confirmed** (source) | `mm/fadvise.c:113-170`: the call first starts asynchronous writeback of the whole file (`__filemap_fdatawrite_range(..., WB_SYNC_NONE)`, :114), then tries to evict; `mapping_evict_folio` (`mm/truncate.c:288-303`) returns 0 for every folio that is dirty or under writeback, so on a file written microseconds earlier essentially nothing is freed |
| "... and drains the per-CPU LRU lists on every core per frame" | **confirmed** (source) | every failed folio is counted (`mm/truncate.c:527-531`) and when any failed the call runs `lru_add_drain_all()` (`mm/fadvise.c:165-168`), which schedules a drain work item on each CPU with pending LRU batches and waits for them (`mm/swap.c:792-886`). It also *deactivates* each failed folio (`deactivate_file_folio`, :528): the pages go to the inactive list and are reclaimed first later |
| "the page cache fills and kswapd reclaims during the take" | **measured**, but **understated** | `pgsteal_kswapd` 3.0-3.45 M pages per writer take = 11.5-13.2 GB. A 3-minute take writes 4500 x 16.9 MB = 74 GB = 19.5 M pages. kswapd therefore freed only **16-18 %** of the pages; the other ~60 GB per take were freed by **direct reclaim** inside whoever allocated (the four writers above all, but also any other thread that needed a page). `take3.sh` recorded `pgscan_direct`, `allocstall_normal` and `allocstall_movable` for every take (its `S()` list), but the report quotes only kswapd and the watermark hits. **Those numbers exist in the Mac session's transcript and should be copied into section 2 before any new take is run.** |
| the drain per frame is what costs the capture frames | **unconfirmed, unlikely alone** | `lru_add_drain_all` costs the other CPUs one short kworker run each (microseconds; concurrent callers coalesce by generation, `mm/swap.c:804-870`). about 25 calls a second (one per written file; four writers share the 25 fps) cannot by themselves hold a nice -5 thread on CPU0 for a 40 ms frame period. The data fit a second mechanism at least as well: with free memory held at the low watermark (`kswapd_low_wmark_hit_quickly` 948-1805 per take: kswapd was re-woken before it could sleep, `mm/vmscan.c:7162-7163`) and the system in a standing direct-reclaim regime, an allocation on libcamera's completion thread, in the UI's GPU path or in the CFE/V4L2 path can enter direct reclaim and wait tens of milliseconds. Nothing recorded on 2026-11-01 says which thread stalled |
| the touch UI was "the last straw" | **probable** | UI stopped: clean under `writer` (#5, #17, #59); UI running: clean under `sync` (#74, #75, #80). The UI's cost is conditional on the memory regime, not a cost of its own |
| `sync` vs `writer` is a real difference, not chance | **measured, confounded by order** | Fisher exact, #69-72 (259 of 18,013) vs #74-75 (0 of 9,014): p = 1e-46; adding #78: p = 7e-67. Not chance under independence. But every `writer` take preceded every `sync` take on that boot, and within `writer` the first take (#69, 1 min uptime, 1 lost) differs from the next three (258 of 13,506; p = 2e-31): **state that builds within a boot is a real effect of its own**, and the A/B must interleave the arms (8.3, test B1) |
| 2026-10-30's reading "the per-frame `sync_file_range` hint is needed" (A1 clean, A2 10 % lost) | **probable: superseded** | in `writer` mode the DONTNEED call already starts writeback of the file (`mm/fadvise.c:114`), so A2 removed a redundant kick, nothing else. A0, A1, A2 ran in that order on one boot: clean, clean, 10 % lost, the same within-boot drift as #69 -> #72, two days earlier on a different Core build (`a40112a`). Two boots, two builds, one pattern: **the drift under `writer` is reproducible and is not a regression since A0** (answers item 7 of section 7). Correct the 2026-10-30 entry's reading once Patrik agrees (append-only log: a new entry) |

### 8.2 The five question groups

**Q1. `sync` vs `writer`: real or chance?** Real, see the table; the open question is *what builds within a boot*. The
night report's own guess ("early in the boot, empty page cache") cannot be the whole story: at 415 MB/s the page cache
of a 4 GB board (about 2.6 GB free for it) fills in about **6 s**, so every take, including the clean first one, runs
almost entirely with a full cache. What differs for the first take of a boot is the headroom above the watermarks when the
take starts (2.7 GB free vs 300 MB) and whatever the previous takes left behind that is not page cache (slab: exFAT
inodes and dentries for 15,000 files per take; Core's and the UI's resident memory; fragmentation). `MemFree before`
in section 2 is the one number that separates the arms before any frame is written: 275-327 MB (`writer`) against
507-788 MB (`sync`) and 2,758 MB (#80). The sound A/B is B1 below; the cheapest extra evidence is the direct-reclaim
and allocstall deltas already recorded.

**Q2. #80 changed two things.** Three things, in fact: the dirty limits (`vm.dirty_bytes` 1 GiB,
`vm.dirty_background_bytes` 256 MiB), `vm.min_free_kbytes` 16 -> 64 MiB (a different knob: it raises every zone's
min/low/high watermark four-fold, `mm/page_alloc.c:6125-6160`, so kswapd starts earlier and direct reclaim starts later),
and the drive (formatted, 1 -> 26 % full). B3 separates them. Note where the fill losses of the past appeared: on
2026-10-26 a 77-81 % full card lost 166-676 frames to **queue-full** (the drive slowed); #78's last minute lost 18 frames
at **capture** with the queue at 1 of 12. A slow drive shows in the queue first, so #78's tail is not simply "the drive
filled up"; a full exFAT volume does cost more metadata work per file (cluster bitmap scans, `exfat_alloc_cluster`,
seen in the 2026-10-30 stack dumps), which is more allocation under a memory regime that was already tight. Fill and
memory are entangled; B3 tests them apart, with the trim state controlled (2026-10-30: untrimmed, not full, is what stalls).

**Q3. Which UI conclusions survive?** For the first target, none is needed: #74, #75 and #80 were clean with the
unmodified GPU preview, peaking and zebra on, no plane, no preview cap. Every UI A/B of 19:46-22:20 compared two
configurations under a drifting memory regime without a same-time control, so none of its deltas (peaking 10.4 % vs
1.7 % off; `cpu` 3.9 %; plane 0 then 1.8-3.4 %; preview held 0) can be read as a UI property. The one robust UI finding
is conditional: "UI drawing + `writer` regime = losses; UI stopped or frozen = none". Decisions in 8.4.

**Q5. What was not considered.**
- *The 1782 Mbps CSI-2 link.* Above the RP1 D-PHY's 1500 Mbps/lane (handbook 2026-08-27: it clamps instead of refusing;
  short takes were clean). A receiver error shows as a discard or overflow in `csi2_errors` (M6), not as a late requeue,
  and would not correlate with memory state. Low prior; one M6 reading during a losing take rules it in or out.
- *The NeoRotary 4 poll* (~570 IRQs/s on CPU0, I2C bus 1): a few microseconds each; no memory interaction; low prior.
  Test only if losses survive B1-B3 (unplug the board for one take).
- *All camera, GPU and display IRQs on CPU0* together with libcamera's thread: moving v3d to CPU2 did not help (#61-62).
  Cheap test if losses survive: `taskset -pc 1 <CameraManager tid>` during a take (no restart), or `/proc/irq/154/smp_affinity`
  for rp1-cfe.
- *The WebUI preview* (Core encodes MJPEG on its own thread when a client is attached): costs frames (#4, #37, #61, #76);
  the scripts refuse with a client. Keep that rule.
- *A0's cleanness* is answered above (8.1, last row): first take of a boot, under `writer`, on 2026-10-30 as on 11-01.
- *What else changed between A0 (`a40112a`) and 11-01 (`64bbbaa`), under the same `writer` mode:* `4abbef0` keeps the raw
  pool warm through the take (queue + workers + 2 copies, about 300 MB resident per take instead of ~100 MB),
  `e55557d` writes an unpacked 16-bit payload in one 16.9 MB `write()` instead of 34 chunks, `d44b177` added the RAM
  buffer mode (`[storage] buffer=ram`, budget 0.6 x MemAvailable - 512 MiB, 1,424 MiB on this board; the 11-01 takes ran
  in `frames` mode: `peak_queue=2/12`). None of these is needed to explain the drift (A2 drifted on `a40112a`), but the
  warm pool and the one-shot `write()` both raise the burst of page allocation per frame; M1/M3 would show it if it matters.
