## 1. The result in one paragraph

The target was 4K Clear HDR 16-bit uncompressed cDNG at 25 fps with **no** lost frames while the touch UI shows the live
picture (peaking and zebra on). The losses were single frames missing at capture (Core's `dropped_gap_estimate`; the
write queue never filled). Most of the evening was spent on the touch UI's GPU work, because stopping or freezing the UI
removed the losses. After a reboot the same setup was clean once and then drifted again, and the drift followed **page-
cache pressure**: Core's DNG writer called `posix_fadvise(DONTNEED)` right after each write, on still-dirty pages, which
frees nothing and drains the per-CPU page lists on every core per frame, while the page cache filled and `kswapd`
reclaimed ~13 GB per 3-minute take. Setting `APERTAR_DNG_DONTNEED=sync` (drop the pages after the once-a-second `fsync`)
took the 3-minute takes to 0 lost and a 10-minute take to 25 of 15,023 (0.17 %, 18 of them in the last minute with the
drive 97 % full); with CineMate's dirty-page limits added (`vm.dirty_bytes` 1 GiB, `dirty_background_bytes` 256 MiB,
`min_free_kbytes` 64 MiB) a 10-minute take on the empty drive lost **0 of 15,025** and kswapd reclaimed nothing (#80). The UI was the last straw on an overloaded memory system, not the root cause (probable). Upstream
Apertar has the same per-frame `DONTNEED` call hard-coded (KB `lessons/upstream-issues.md` #44).

## 8. Inspection (2026-11-02, cloud session): does the page-cache claim hold, and what to test next

Written from the desk (no Pi) by the cloud session that also wrote `README.md` §1-4 of this folder, after reading
sections 1-7 above, every take in `takes-2026-11-01.md` and the raw journal, the hardware-log entries of 2026-10-26, 10-30,
10-31 and 11-01, Core `drop-frames` @ `adac040`, UI `drop-frames` @ `c13b846`, and the kernel 6.12 source
(`raspberrypi/linux` `rpi-6.12.y` @ `5bd564a`: `mm/`, `drivers/media/platform/raspberrypi/rp1_cfe/`, `arch/arm64/configs/bcm2712_defconfig`).
Five independent examiners (statistics, kernel, Core code, UI commits, co-factors) and three critics worked over the
same sources; their full reports are in the session's scratchpad and what survived them is here. Status words as in
the KB. **Page size: the 2712 kernel runs 16 KiB pages (`bcm2712_defconfig:50`); every page count below assumes it;
`getconf PAGESIZE` on the Pi confirms.**

### 8.1 Verdict

The **fix is right; the stated mechanism is one third right; the proof is not finished.** Three rows of the night
report's reasoning need correcting before anyone builds on them.

| Part of the claim | Status | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `writer` mode's `posix_fadvise(DONTNEED)` on a just-written DNG "frees nothing" | **confirmed** (source) | `mm/fadvise.c:113-114`: the call first starts asynchronous writeback of the whole file (`__filemap_fdatawrite_range(..., WB_SYNC_NONE)`, `nr_to_write = LONG_MAX`, `mm/filemap.c:421-432`), so every dirty page is submitted before the eviction pass; `mm/truncate.c:293-294` refuses dirty and under-writeback folios. Nothing that was just written can be freed by the call |
| "... and drains the per-CPU LRU lists on every core per frame" | **refuted** (source) | the drain runs only when the eviction pass counted failures (`nr_failed`, `mm/fadvise.c:165-168`). Folios under writeback are skipped by `find_lock_entries` before they can be counted (`mm/filemap.c:2123-2125`), and the fdatawrite just put every page of the file under writeback. So `nr_failed` is 0 and `lru_add_drain_all()` is not reached; the pages are neither evicted nor deactivated and stay on the LRU as clean file pages for reclaim. The README knob row (`adac040:README.md:324`), the `0ed07ea`/`adac040` commit messages, upstream issue #44 and the 2026-11-01 hardware-log entry all state the drain; correct them. (The drain also could not have cost a frame: one short nice-0 kworker run per CPU, about 25 calls a second.) |
| "the page cache fills and kswapd reclaims during the take" | **measured** | `pgsteal_kswapd` 3.02-3.45 M pages x 16 KiB = **49-55 GB per 3-minute writer take, 65-74 % of the 74-76 GB written**; `kswapd_low_wmark_hit_quickly` 948-1805 = kswapd reached `high`, tried to sleep and was re-woken within 100 ms 5-10 times a second (`mm/vmscan.c:7104-7119, 7162-7163`): the system sat on the low watermark for the whole take. `sync`: 47-58 k pages, 8-11 hits. The remaining 20-26 GB per writer take were freed by direct reclaim in the allocating threads or by later DONTNEED calls finding already-written pages: `take3.sh` printed `pgscan_direct`, `allocstall_normal` and `allocstall_movable` for every take (its `S()` list) but section 2 does not quote them. **Those numbers are in the Mac session's transcript; copy them into section 2 before any new take (M1).** |
| the memory regime is what costs the capture frames | **unconfirmed** | nothing recorded says which thread stalled. The kernel offers two stalls to a user thread in this regime, both needing the thread to allocate a page: direct reclaim (bounded, ms) and `throttle_direct_reclaim` below `min/2` (unbounded until kswapd refills; `mm/vmscan.c` `throttle_direct_reclaim`). The default watermark window on this board is tiny: `min_free_kbytes` 16384 gives min/low/high = 16/20/24 MiB (`mm/page_alloc.c:6125-6180`), i.e. **10 ms of the 415 MB/s stream between "kswapd wakes" and "allocations reclaim for themselves"**. Core's completion thread allocates about three small heap blocks per frame and no pages in steady state (`CameraController.cpp` around `requestComplete`, T25 moved the copy off it), so the path to a 40 ms hole runs through a page allocation somewhere (the UI's GEM/shmem buffers are the likeliest, mechanism B below), through CPU0 competition with a kswapd that is awake all take (C), or through a starved CFE node (E). M3-M6 tell them apart |
| the touch UI was "the last straw" | **probable, as an interaction** | UI stopped or frozen under `writer`: clean at any uptime (#5, #17, #27, #59). UI drawing under `sync`: clean (#74, #75, #80). The claim that holds is "writer-regime reclaim AND the UI drawing"; neither alone has lost a frame |
| `sync` vs `writer` is a real difference, not chance | **suggestive, not settled** | the frame-level Fisher tests (p = 1e-46 and smaller) are invalid: losses are clustered (per-take variance/mean = 66; 18 of #78's 25 in one minute), so the unit is the take. Take-level exact test, 4 writer vs 3 sync takes: one-sided **p = 0.057**, two-sided 0.11; with #80, 0.029 / 0.057. The arms overlap (#69, writer, 0.02 % sits below #78, sync, 0.17 %). The kernel counters are the strong part (50-70x, no overlap): they prove the mode changed the kernel's behaviour, not that the change caused the gaps. Every writer take preceded every sync take; the only other thing that changed between #72 and #74 is the Core restart at 22:46 (in boot 1 a restart did not help, #64, n = 1). Uptime and fill both ran *against* `sync` (later, fuller) |
| "the evening drifted with uptime" | **not supported** | within #69-#72: 1, 34, 153, 71 is not monotone (#72 < #71; n = 4, p = 0.33). Across boot 1 the baseline takes 19:09-21:03 are flat (7.5 %, slope 1.2 %/h, p = 0.25) and the rise is a **step** to 11-18 % between 21:03 and 22:06, after the 21:50 format (drive 1-35 % full, trimmed) with a Core restart at 22:14 in between: fill is excluded as its cause; a regime change around 21:50-22:06 fits as well as a drift. What *does* reproduce: the first take of a freshly started Core process is clean under `writer` (#69; A0) and later ones are not (#70-#72; A2) |
| why A0 (2026-10-30) was clean (section 7 item 7: "early in the boot, empty cache") | **refuted** | A0 ran at least 7.4 h into its boot (the UI had been up since 01:04, A0 at 08:30; hardware-log 2026-11-01 first entry) and the cache fills in about 6 s at 415 MB/s on this board, so an empty cache cannot carry a take. A0, A1, A2 were each the first take after a Core restart (the matrix restarted Core per arm) and went clean, clean, 10 % lost; on 11-01 the second take (#70) already lost 34. The two nights agree only that something builds under `writer` within a boot; they disagree on when. The 2026-10-30 reading "the per-frame `sync_file_range` hint is needed" (A1 vs A2) is **probably superseded**: `a40112a` already had the per-frame DONTNEED (`CdngEncoder.cpp:1780` there), which starts the file's writeback itself, so A2 removed a redundant kick and its 10 % is the same within-boot effect. A correcting hardware-log entry once Patrik agrees |
| `min_free_kbytes` 64 MiB and the dirty limits were "CineMate's" | **wrong attribution; the dirty limits are inert here** | cinemate's automounter sets `vm.dirty_*` only (`storage-automount.py:319-336`); `min_free_kbytes` is the 2026-11-01 session's own knob. And the dirty limits cannot have mattered: with per-frame `sync_file_range` the measured peak `Dirty` was 11 MB (A0) against a freerun ceiling of about 435 MB (defaults) or 640 MiB (#80), so `balance_dirty_pages` never paused a writer under either setting (`mm/page-writeback.c:377-446, 837-841, 1769`), and it never touches the capture thread, which dirties nothing. Of #80's changes, `min_free_kbytes` (window 8 -> 32 MiB, 19 -> 77 ms of stream) is the one that engages (**probable**; B3 tests it alone). #80 also started with an empty cache (the 23:13 format freed the clips' pages: `MemFree` 2,758 MB), a third difference, small in effect (6 s) |

Two more facts the night did not state. **Every post-reboot take, writer and sync alike, ran the RAM write buffer**
(`queue_budget=1.40-1.59 GB` on every `recording stats` line in the raw journal; `peak_queue=2/12` is printed in frames
either way). It is not the discriminator, but it is a mode A0 did not have (`d44b177`), and from its formula
`MemAvailable` at take start was 3.2-3.5 GB in every take: the arms differed in `MemFree` (275-327 vs 507-788 MB), not in
what the kernel counted as reclaimable. And **the imx585 driver changed on 2026-11-01** (`24172e6` -> `cd548ef`, the
cinemate-port mode tables, installed before 19:09): no 16-bit 25 fps take has run with today's Core and the old driver.

### 8.2 The five question groups

**Q1. `sync` vs `writer`: real or chance?** Suggestive (take-level p 0.057), with the kernel counters saying the mode
really changed the regime. The open question is what builds within a Core process under `writer`: not the cache (6 s),
but something that makes the second and later takes lose where the first does not. Candidates with their reading, all
**unconfirmed**: slab left by 15,000 exFAT files per take (`Slab`, `SReclaimable`, `/proc/slabinfo` exfat/dentry/buffer_head

**Q5. What was not considered.**
- *The imx585 driver* (`24172e6` -> `cd548ef` on 11-01): untested as a co-factor; T1/T2 below cover it.
- *The CSI-2 link at 1782 Mbps* (891 MHz x 2): the receiver programs its 1500 Mbps calibration bin and keeps streaming
  (`rp1_cfe/dphy.c:99-128`); a link error shows as LEN/UNMATCHED discards in `csi2_errors` and cannot correlate with memory
  or UI state; a receiver **FIFO overflow** (OVLF) is the one link-side counter that would. The counter needs
  `rp1_cfe.track_csi2_errors=1` at boot, resets on read, and has never been read during a losing take (2026-10-26: all zero
  under `dd` loads). A control exists: `link-frequency=720000000` (1440 Mbps, in spec) gives 4K Clear HDR 16-bit **exactly
  25.0 fps** from the driver's HMAX table (`imx585.c:255-302`; probable, the probe prints the exact value), with no headroom.
- *CPU0's interrupts*: rp1-cfe (154), USB and I2C are MSI-X children of RP1 whose affinity call is forwarded to a PCIe MSI
  domain declared `MSI_FLAG_NO_AFFINITY` with no `irq_set_affinity` handler (`drivers/mfd/rp1.c:144-157`,
  `drivers/pci/controller/pcie-brcmstb.c:664-674, 720-724`, `kernel/irq/msi.c:663-670`): **do not write
  `/proc/irq/154/smp_affinity`** (a NULL handler is dereferenced). What can move: the v3d GIC lines (done, no gain), the
  capture thread (`taskset -pc 1 $T`, live, no restart), kswapd (`taskset -pc 3 $(pgrep kswapd0)`), the NeoRotary poll
  (unplug the board or `poll_ms` 1000 for one take; about 570 IRQs/s, microseconds each, very low prior).
- *The two preview feeds*: the WebUI's 1280x720 -> 640x360 downsample runs on libcamera's thread while a browser is
  attached (`ApertarCoreApp.cpp` around the MJPEG publisher; the JPEG encode is on its own thread): a real completion-thread
  cost, which is why the scripts refuse with a client on `:8000`. The touch UI's feed is not: after a one-time dma-buf
  registration per slot, a frame costs the completion thread a descriptor hand-off, and the publisher thread sends a
  ~120-byte line non-blocking to each client, dropping a stalled client rather than waiting
  (`CameraController.cpp` `publishPreviewFrame`, `ApertarCoreApp.cpp` preview publisher, `ControlServer.cpp`). So a stalled
  UI cannot block the capture thread through the feed (**confirmed** from source); mechanism B needs another coupling.
- *What else changed between A0 and 11-01 under the same `writer` mode*: Core `a40112a` -> `64bbbaa` (warm raw pool kept
  through the take, `4abbef0`; one 16.9 MB `write()` instead of 34 chunks, `e55557d`; RAM buffer mode, pre-roll and trim,
  `d44b177`), UI `ea44bd7` -> `c13b846` (14 commits), the driver, and the drive protocol (A0 cooled to 34 C, one take; 11-01
  four takes 4 min apart).
